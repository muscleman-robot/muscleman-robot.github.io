Overview

Heavy lifting breaks pure imitation.

Humanoid motion trackers now reproduce diverse human motion on real hardware, yet whole-body loco-manipulation remains limited to light objects and largely non-dexterous interaction. Heavy prehensile lifting exposes the limits of pure imitation: human demonstrations provide only kinematic references, and under load the embodiment gap between human and humanoid drives the robot's weakest actuators into torque saturation and thermal shutdown.

MuscleMan constructs dynamically feasible references from human videos, couples separately pretrained whole-body and dexterous-hand priors in a dual-stream policy, and trains it to avoid overloading and overheating its motors under a progressive payload curriculum. On a Unitree G1 with Dex3-1 hands it autonomously clean-and-jerks a commercial barbell from 6.8 kg up to 20.4 kg.

Real hardware

From the empty bar to 45 lb.

Every lift is autonomous and shown at real-time speed. The bar alone weighs 6.8 kg; a spotter stands behind the robot for safety.

When it goes wrong

Heavy lifts fail in physical ways: a motor overheats, a grip slips, the bar runs away.

Method

Human motion as a prior, not a target.

A

Load-aware reference

We reconstruct an Olympic gold medalist's competition clean and jerk from video (CARI4D), retarget it to the G1 with Dex3-1 hands (object-conditioned GMR), and refine it to minimize the normalized peak joint torque under load, keeping only references that a physics verifier confirms are feasible.

B

Dual-stream policy

A body stream (warm-started from the SONIC whole-body controller, 29 joints) and a hand stream (pretrained on floating Dex3 hands, 14 finger joints) are coupled by a single zero-initialized residual attention block, then fine-tuned together on the lift.

C

Load- and thermal-aware training

PPO with object-tracking rewards, a smooth-max penalty on the most loaded motor, and a motor thermal model fitted to hardware logs (penalty above 100 °C, shutdown at 130 °C), under a payload curriculum that grows in 0.5 kg steps.

Imitation alone does not lift.

Without reference refinement no lift succeeds at any load. At 11.3 kg, GRAIL tracks the body reference more closely (0.10 vs 0.42 rad joint error) yet completes no lifts.

Heat is the hidden limit.

Without thermal training, success falls to 0% at 11.3 kg. At that load, thermal training lowers the mean peak motor temperature from 97.8 to 76.9 °C and overheat shutdowns from 12.5% to 3.1%.

Pretraining and the curriculum unlock load.

Without task pretraining the policy lifts only the 0.5 kg load; without the payload curriculum it lifts nothing.
