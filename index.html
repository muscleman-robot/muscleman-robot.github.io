Abstract

Humanoid motion trackers now reproduce diverse human motion on real hardware, yet whole-body loco-manipulation remains limited to light objects and largely non-dexterous interaction. Heavy prehensile lifting exposes the limits of pure imitation: human demonstrations provide only kinematic references, and under load the embodiment gap between human and humanoid drives the robot's weakest actuators into torque saturation and thermal shutdown.

We present MuscleMan, a whole-body dexterous loco-manipulation framework that constructs dynamically feasible references from human videos, couples separately pretrained whole-body and dexterous-hand priors in a dual-stream policy, and trains it to avoid overloading and overheating its motors under a progressive payload curriculum. On hardware, MuscleMan exceeds the manufacturer's payload limits and clean-and-jerks 11.3 kg with a commercial barbell under Olympic-style judging, making this the heaviest autonomous lift by a humanoid of its size.

Method

Copying a human lift overloads the robot's weakest joints, so MuscleMan adapts the demonstration to the robot in three stages.

Load-aware reference

Reconstruct the human lift from video, retarget it to the robot, repair the grasp, and verify that no joint exceeds its torque limit under load.

Dual-stream policy

A body stream and a dexterous-hand stream are pretrained separately on suitable data, then coupled through a zero-initialized attention block.

Load- and thermal-aware training

A torque penalty on the most loaded joint, a per-motor thermal model fitted to hardware logs, and a progressive payload curriculum.
